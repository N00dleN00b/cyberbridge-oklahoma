# Module 2: Understanding Security Controls — Lesson Plan

**Ages:** 11–18 and adults · **Time:** 50 minutes · **Group size:** 10–35 · **Prep:** 10 minutes
**Educator version:** see `../../workshops/workshop-educators.md`

## Big idea
A **security control** is anything that protects information: a lock, a rule, a habit, a setting. Organizations like schools and hospitals stack many controls so one failure doesn't become a disaster. Most attacks target **people**, which means people are one of the most important controls. You can be a **protector, not a bystander**.

## Why this matters in Oklahoma
Connect every part of this lesson to three real kinds of local threats (use recent, public news examples from Oklahoma or nearby states; check the facts before presenting):
1. **Phishing attacks on school districts** — one staff member's click can expose student records or shut down systems.
2. **Data breaches at hospitals** — patient records are valuable to criminals; ransomware has forced hospitals to divert ambulances.
3. **Social engineering scams targeting families** — fake toll/package texts, grandparent scams, AI voice clones, fake job offers.

## Learning objectives
Students will be able to:
1. Define a security control and give examples of **preventive**, **detective** and **corrective** controls.
2. Sort controls into **technical**, **administrative** (rules/training) and **physical**.
3. Recognize at least four red flags of phishing (the human control).
4. Create a strong passphrase and explain why multi-factor authentication (MFA) matters.
5. Explain how updates and backups limit ransomware damage.
6. Know what to do after a mistake.

## Vocabulary

| Word | Definition |
|---|---|
| Security control | Any safeguard that protects information or systems: a technology, a rule, or something physical |
| Preventive control | Stops something bad before it happens (MFA, locked doors, training) |
| Detective control | Notices something bad happening (alerts, logs, "new sign-in" notifications) |
| Corrective control | Fixes or recovers after something bad happens (backups, incident response plans) |
| Defense in depth | Using layers of controls so one failure isn't enough for an attacker |
| Phishing | A message pretending to be trustworthy to trick you into clicking, paying or sharing information |
| Ransomware | Malicious software that locks files and demands payment |
| Multi-factor authentication (MFA) | Needing a second proof, like a code or app approval, besides your password |

## Lesson flow

### 1. Hook: Protect the castle (5 min)
Draw a castle. Ask: *"How did castles protect the treasure?"* (walls, moat, guards, gate, a lookout, a plan if the wall is breached).
Map each to modern controls: walls/moat = firewall; gate + guard checking ID = passwords + MFA; lookout = alerts/monitoring; trained guards = security awareness; rebuild plan = backups.
Say: *"My job as a Security Control Assessor is basically checking whether the castle's defenses actually work. Today you'll learn to do it too."*

### 2. Types of controls (7 min)
Teach the two ways to sort controls:
- **When they act:** Preventive → Detective → Corrective
- **What they are:** Technical (MFA, antivirus, encryption) · Administrative (rules, training, policies) · Physical (locks, badges, cameras)
Key point: **defense in depth.** No single control is perfect, so we stack them.

### 3. Activity A: Control Sort (8 min)
Groups sort control cards into the grid. See `activity.md`, Part A. Then: *"Which control would have stopped a phishing attack on a school district? A ransomware attack on a hospital?"*

### 4. The human control: spotting phishing (5 min)
Teach **SLAM**: check the **S**ender, **L**inks, **A**ttachments, **M**essage.
Red flags: ⏰ urgency · 🎁 too good to be true · 🔗 mismatched links · 🔑 asks for passwords, codes or payment · 👤 strange sender.
Rule: **Stop. Think. Check it another way.**

### 5. Activity B: Phishing Spotter (10 min)
Game or cards (`activity.md`, Part B). Debrief: which one fooled the most people and why?

### 6. Controls you control (8 min)
- **Passphrases:** `Tractor-Pancake-Thunder-Okra` beats `Okla2010!`. Never reuse passwords.
- **MFA:** turn it on for email, social media and games. Never share a login code.
- **Updates** (preventive) and **backups** (corrective) take the power away from ransomware.
- **Privacy settings:** decide what you share. Pet names and birthdays are common security-question answers.

### 7. Be the Protector (5 min)
- **Made a mistake?** Tell a trusted adult, change the password, turn on MFA, report it. That's your personal incident response plan (a corrective control!).
- **Teach-Back:** each student commits to teaching one family member one control this week (handout).

### 8. Exit ticket (2 min)
`quiz.md`.
