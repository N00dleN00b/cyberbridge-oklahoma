# Module 3: Pathways to Cybersecurity Careers — Lesson Plan

**Ages:** 13–18 · **Time:** 45 minutes · **Prep:** 15 minutes (prepare your own story)

## Big idea
There is a path into cybersecurity and AI from where you are right now, whatever your school offers, whatever your family background, wherever you live in Oklahoma.

## Learning objectives
Students will be able to:
1. Name at least three cybersecurity or AI roles and what each does day to day.
2. Identify one free next step they could take this year.
3. Name at least one route that doesn't require already knowing how to code.
4. Recognize that people from backgrounds like theirs work in this field, and that programs like Gilman make study abroad possible for students like them.

## Materials
- Slides (`slides-outline.md`)
- Career cards (`activity.md`), one set per group
- Handout with free resources (`handout.md`)

## Lesson flow

### 1. Hook: My story (7 min)
Tell your own story honestly and briefly. Bristie's beats are below; other facilitators can share their own story or tell hers in the third person.
- Growing up in a low-income Oklahoma household; high school offered no cybersecurity classes
- First found cybersecurity in college, surrounded by classmates who seemed to have a head start
- Almost quit in the first semester, until a professor from a similar background said resilience, not pedigree, determines success
- Worked as a Security Control Assessor at OCII and a GenCyber teaching assistant, in plain words: *"I checked whether organizations were actually protecting their systems and data the way they're supposed to."* Now starting a new career opportunity in the field
- Studying in China as a Gilman Scholar (Shanghai University of Sport, China University of Petroleum (Beijing), sample AI lectures, the Great Wall): *"I never thought someone like me would study abroad. A scholarship made it possible."*

End with: *"Resilience matters more than where you started."*

### 2. What does cybersecurity actually look like? (8 min)
Bust myths:
- ❌ "It's all hoodies and hacking in the dark." ✅ Most jobs are teamwork, problem-solving, writing and explaining.
- ❌ "You have to be a math genius." ✅ Curiosity, persistence and communication matter a lot.
- ❌ "You need to already know how to code." ✅ Many roles (governance, risk, compliance, assessment, awareness training) start without heavy coding.
- ❌ "Those jobs aren't in Oklahoma." ✅ Hospitals, banks, tribal nations, energy companies, Tinker Air Force Base, state agencies, universities and schools all need security people.

### 3. Activity: Career Match (15 min)
See `activity.md`. Groups match job cards to "a day in the life" descriptions, then pick the role they'd most want to try and why.

### 4. Your next step (10 min)
Walk through free starting points (on handout). Each student circles **one** to try this month.
- **CyberPatriot** (national youth cyber defense competition; teams form through schools, clubs, JROTC, Scouts and others)
- **picoCTF** (free, beginner-friendly security puzzles from Carnegie Mellon)
- **TryHackMe / free beginner rooms** and **Cisco Networking Academy free intro courses**
- **Oklahoma CareerTech** technology centers and community colleges offering cyber and IT programs
- **Scholarships:** CyberCorps Scholarship for Service; and for study abroad, the **Gilman Scholarship** for students who receive a Federal Pell Grant
- **Talk to someone:** a school counselor, a local cyber nonprofit, or Bristie Rahman on LinkedIn

### 5. Study abroad is for you, too (3 min)
Use the Gilman segment from `../../workshops/gilman-segment.md`.

### 6. Exit ticket (2 min)
`quiz.md`, plus one sentence: *"One thing I could see myself doing is ____."*

## Note on salaries and job numbers
If you share pay or growth figures, pull them fresh from the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics Occupational Outlook Handbook ("Information Security Analysts") the week of your workshop and cite the year. Don't use old numbers from slides.
