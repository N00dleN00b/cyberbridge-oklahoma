# Module 1: AI in Everyday Life — Lesson Plan

**Ages:** 11–18 · **Time:** 45 minutes · **Group size:** 10–35 · **Prep:** 10 minutes

## Learning objectives

Students will be able to:
1. Identify at least three places they already use AI (recommendations, filters, voice assistants, chatbots, spam filters, maps).
2. Explain in one sentence how machine learning works: *a computer finds patterns in lots of examples and uses them to make guesses.*
3. Describe one way AI can be wrong or unfair, and one habit for checking AI output.
4. Connect AI to their personal data: apps learn from what they click, watch and share.

## Materials

- Slides (see `slides-outline.md`) or a whiteboard
- Printed "Train the Robot" cards (see `activity.md`), one set per group of 4
- Handout (`handout.md`), one per student
- Optional: a device with a free chatbot to demo live (teacher only)

## Vocabulary

| Word | Kid-friendly definition |
|---|---|
| Artificial intelligence (AI) | Computer programs that do tasks we usually think need human thinking, like recognizing faces or answering questions |
| Machine learning (ML) | A way of building AI where the computer learns patterns from lots of examples instead of following hand-written rules |
| Training data | The examples an AI learns from |
| Bias | When an AI's guesses are unfair or wrong for some groups, often because its training data was missing them |
| Hallucination | When a chatbot states something false as if it's true |

## Lesson flow

### 1. Hook: "Is it AI?" (5 min)
Read each item and have students stand up if they think it uses AI, stay seated if not:
- Your TikTok/YouTube "For You" feed → **AI** (recommendation system)
- A calculator → **not AI** (fixed rules)
- Your phone unlocking with your face → **AI** (face recognition)
- Snapchat/Instagram face filters → **AI**
- Google Maps rerouting you around traffic → **AI** (predicts traffic from data)
- A light switch → **not AI**
- Your email spam folder → **AI**

Say: *"Most of you use AI dozens of times a day without noticing. Today we'll figure out how it works, and how it learns from you."*

### 2. Mini-lesson: How machines learn (8 min)
Use the dog vs. cat example:
- **Old way (rules):** "If it has pointy ears, it's a cat." Breaks immediately (German Shepherds!).
- **Machine learning way:** Show the computer 10,000 labeled photos of dogs and cats. It finds patterns on its own. Then it guesses on new photos.
- Key point: **the AI is only as good as its examples.** If it never saw a hairless cat, it'll get hairless cats wrong.

Connect to them: *"Every video you watch to the end, every like, every skip is a training example. Your feed is a model trained on you."*

### 3. Activity: Train the Robot (15 min)
See `activity.md`. Groups act as a "machine learning model," learn from example cards, then get tested on new cards, including tricky ones they weren't trained on. Debrief on why the robot failed.

### 4. When AI gets it wrong (7 min)
Three short discussion examples:
- **Chatbots make things up.** They predict likely-sounding words, not true facts. Always check important answers against a real source.
- **Face recognition can be less accurate for some groups** when training data doesn't include enough people who look like them. Ask: *"Who might be missing from the training data?"*
- **Deepfakes and AI scams.** AI can clone voices and make fake images. Families should have a "code word" for real emergencies (this sets up Module 2).

### 5. Global connection (3 min)
Briefly share something from the creator's time in China (see `../global-perspectives-china/`): she attended sample AI lectures at Chinese universities, and phone-based payments and AI features are part of everyday life there. Ask: *"Would you want that here? What would worry you?"* There's no single right answer; the goal is thinking critically.

### 6. Exit ticket (5 min)
Hand out `quiz.md` questions (or ask them aloud). Collect or tally hands.

## Unplugged / no-device version
Everything above works without devices. Skip the live chatbot demo.

## Short version (25 min)
Hook (5) → Mini-lesson (7) → Train the Robot, one round (8) → Exit ticket (5).

## Differentiation
- **Younger students:** focus on "Is it AI?" and Train the Robot; skip bias discussion depth.
- **Older students:** add a debate: *"Should schools use AI to grade essays?"*
