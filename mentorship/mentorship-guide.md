# Mentorship Guide

From the project proposal: *"As a first-generation college student and woman in STEM, I want students who feel invisible to see themselves in my story."* This guide helps anyone, including the creator, turn that into real conversations about study abroad and cyber careers.

## Who to mentor
- Students who come up to you after workshops or peer sessions
- College students on your campus considering study abroad (via the study abroad office, first-gen student groups, cyber/CS clubs, women in STEM groups)
- Peers and younger students interested in cyber careers

## Three kinds of conversations

### 1. "Can I study abroad?" (15–30 min)
- Share your path honestly: what scared you, what helped
- Explain Gilman basics and point them to gilmanscholarship.org and their study abroad office
- Ask: Do you receive (or expect to receive) a Federal Pell Grant? Have you filed FAFSA this year?
- Next step together: pick one action (visit the study abroad office, attend an info session, look at programs)

### 2. Gilman essay feedback (30–45 min)
- Read their draft; give feedback on clarity, specificity and their own voice
- Help them brainstorm a Follow-on Service Project idea that fits *their* skills
- Don't write it for them; the essay must be their own work

### 3. "Can I do cybersecurity?" (15–30 min)
- Share Module 3 resources (CyberPatriot, picoCTF, free courses, CareerTech, CyberCorps SFS)
- Point them to GenCyber camps, local cyber nonprofits and campus clubs where appropriate
- Pick one first step

## Boundaries and safety
- Meet minors only in group or public settings with a supervising adult (school, library, program staff); don't DM minors privately on social media. Follow your host's youth-protection policies
- Keep mentee info private; log conversations without names

## Log it
Jot down each conversation (date, format, topic, no names) in `../impact/workshop-tracker.csv` or a notes app.
