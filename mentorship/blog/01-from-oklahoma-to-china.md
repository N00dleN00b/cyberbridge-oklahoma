# From Oklahoma to China: Why a First-Gen Student Studied Abroad

*By Bristie Rahman · Part of my Gilman Scholarship Follow-on Service Project*

I grew up in a low-income household in Oklahoma. My high school didn't offer cybersecurity classes. While students in other places were learning to code, my classmates and I weren't. College was something my parents never got the chance to try, and studying in another country wasn't even on my list of possibilities.

I didn't find cybersecurity until college. My first semester, I almost quit. My classmates seemed to have a head start I'd never catch up on. Then a professor who came from a background like mine told me something I still carry: resilience, not pedigree, determines success.

So I kept going. I went on to work as a Security Control Assessor at the Oklahoma Center for Information and Infrastructure (OCII), helping schools and small organizations figure out whether their defenses actually work, and I served as a teaching assistant for a GenCyber bootcamp. Then I did something younger me wouldn't have believed: I studied in China as a **Benjamin A. Gilman International Scholar**.

## Where I went

My program took me to more than one city. I attended **Shanghai University of Sport** and **China University of Petroleum (Beijing)**, learning about a range of topics and sitting in on sample AI lectures. Outside the classroom, I walked through the **Forbidden City**, the old imperial palace at the heart of Beijing, and stood on the **Great Wall**. For someone whose high school didn't offer a single cybersecurity class, sitting in an AI lecture on the other side of the world was a moment I won't forget.

## What a scholarship made possible

The Gilman Scholarship is a U.S. Department of State program for undergraduates who receive a Federal Pell Grant. It exists for students like me: first-generation students, students with financial need, and students in fields like STEM who don't usually see themselves in study abroad photos.

## What I brought home

Cybersecurity and AI don't stop at borders. Seeing how technology is built, used and regulated in another country gave me a bigger picture of the field I work in, and made me better at explaining it.

So I turned what I learned into **CyberBridge Oklahoma**: a free, open curriculum on AI in everyday life, security controls and cybersecurity careers, with ready-to-run workshops for students, educators, families and college students. Every workshop includes a segment on study abroad and the Gilman Scholarship, because I want students who feel invisible to know these opportunities are for them too.

Since coming home, I've also moved for a new career opportunity in cybersecurity. That made in-person workshops across the state harder, so I built everything so that teachers, librarians and outreach teams can run it without me.

## If you're reading this and thinking "not for me"

I thought that too. If you're a Pell Grant student, first-gen, or in STEM, look up the Gilman Scholarship at gilmanscholarship.org and visit your study abroad office. And reach out to me on LinkedIn. I'll tell you everything I wish someone had told me.
