# Run of Show: College Peer Session

**Length:** 45–60 minutes · **Audience:** college students (classmates, student organizations, first-gen and STEM groups) · **Devices:** phones are enough

This is the most direct way to meet the Gilman goal: college students are the people who can actually apply for the Gilman Scholarship. Pair the study abroad story with a hands-on cyber/AI demo so it's worth showing up for.

| Time | Segment | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | Welcome: "Who here thinks study abroad isn't for them?" | `gilman-segment.md`, step 1 |
| 0:03 | **My story: Oklahoma → China** | First-gen, low-income, almost quit; studying in China at Shanghai University of Sport and China University of Petroleum (Beijing); sample AI lectures; Forbidden City and Great Wall |
| 0:13 | **What China taught me about digital life** | Global Perspectives lesson, steps 1–2: payments, super apps, data laws, convenience vs. privacy |
| 0:23 | **Phishing Spotter on your phones** | Everyone plays; compare scores; debrief the trickiest one |
| 0:33 | **AI in everyday life, quick version** | Module 1: how AI learns, hallucinations, AI voice scams, family code word |
| 0:40 | **How to get there: Gilman + study abroad** | `gilman-segment.md`, steps 2–4: Pell Grant + FAFSA, study abroad office, essay tips, Follow-on Service Project idea |
| 0:50 | Q&A + offer to read Gilman essay drafts | `../mentorship/mentorship-guide.md` |
| 0:55 | Share the repo link; invite people to connect on LinkedIn | — |

## Where to host it
- A classroom with a professor's permission (10 minutes at the start of class works too)
- A student organization meeting (cyber club, CS club, first-gen students, women in STEM, cultural organizations)
- The study abroad office's info sessions
- Online (Zoom/Teams) for friends and classmates who can't meet in person

## Afterward
Write down: date, where, how many attended, what questions people asked about study abroad. You'll need these for the Gilman report.
