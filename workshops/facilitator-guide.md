# Facilitator Guide

For anyone running a CyberBridge Oklahoma workshop: the creator, outreach teams, teachers, librarians or volunteers. No cybersecurity background required.

## Before the workshop
- [ ] Confirm date, room, number of students, ages and whether devices/Wi-Fi are available
- [ ] Ask the host whether photos are allowed; bring photo permission forms if you'll take any
- [ ] Choose the format: `workshop-peer-session.md` (college peers), `workshop-school.md` (class period), `workshop-library.md` (family night) or `workshop-educators.md` (staff)
- [ ] Print handouts and quiz/exit tickets (optional: pre/post surveys in `../impact/pre-post-survey.md`)
- [ ] If devices are available, test the Phishing Spotter link on the site's network (some school filters block new sites; bring printed cards as backup)
- [ ] Load slides; put the repo / Gilman QR code on the last slide
- [ ] Prepare the 60–90 second Gilman / study abroad story (`gilman-segment.md`); photos optional

## During
- **Start with a story, not a definition.** People remember stories.
- **Make it interactive every 5–7 minutes**: stand up/sit down, pair talk, games.
- **No shaming.** If a student says they fell for a scam, thank them for sharing; it helps others. Plenty of adults and professionals have too.
- **Defense only.** Teach how to recognize and stop attacks, never how to carry them out.
- **Protect privacy.** Don't ask students to share passwords, real accounts or personal details. Use fictional examples.
- **Always include the Gilman segment** (`gilman-segment.md`).

## After
- [ ] Collect exit tickets (and surveys, if used)
- [ ] Log the date, place and headcount in `../impact/workshop-tracker.csv` the same day
- [ ] Jot 3 quick notes: one thing that worked, one to fix, one memorable student quote (no names)
- [ ] Send the host a thank-you
- [ ] Optional: post about it; no student faces without permission

## Accessibility and inclusion
- Large-print handouts available on request; read text aloud
- Offer handouts in Spanish where needed (community translation welcome; see CONTRIBUTING)
- Use examples from students' lives: gaming, sports, part-time jobs, farm/ranch tech, family group chats
- Be mindful that some students don't have their own phone or home internet; never assume
